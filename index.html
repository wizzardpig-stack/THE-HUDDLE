We are not the same.

Different communities got here by different roads. Solidarity doesn't require pretending those roads were identical, and nobody here is scoring who had it worse.

Black communities

  1. 1619Enslaved Africans are brought to the English colony of Virginia.
  2. 1877–1965Jim Crow laws enforce segregation across the South.
  3. 1930sFederal HOLC maps grade Black neighborhoods "hazardous," steering lending away for decades.
  4. NowHomeownership and wealth gaps that trace back to who was allowed to buy where.

Native communities

  1. 1830The Indian Removal Act forces nations off homelands east of the Mississippi.
  2. 1855The Treaty of Point Elliott takes Duwamish and Suquamish land, including what is now Seattle.
  3. 1879 onFederal boarding schools separate children from their families and languages.
  4. NowNations still in court over treaty rights to land, water, and fishing.

Latino and Hispanic communities

  1. 1848The Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo moves the border over families already living in the Southwest.
  2. 1930sDeportation drives expel Mexican Americans, U.S. citizens among them.
  3. 1942–1964The Bracero Program brings Mexican farmworkers on contracts with few protections.
  4. NowCitizenship, language, and regional histories that differ widely inside one label.

Asian communities

  1. 1882The Chinese Exclusion Act bars Chinese laborers from entering the country.
  2. 1886White mobs force most of Seattle's Chinese residents out of the city.
  3. 1942Executive Order 9066 leads to the incarceration of about 120,000 Japanese Americans.
  4. NowSome of the widest income gaps of any U.S. group, hidden inside one label.

Pacific Islander communities

  1. 1893U.S.-backed businessmen overthrow the Hawaiian Kingdom.
  2. 1898The U.S. takes Guam after the Spanish-American War.
  3. 1946–1958The U.S. conducts 67 nuclear tests in the Marshall Islands.
  4. NowMarshallese and Micronesian families building lives in U.S. towns, far from islands shaped by U.S. policy.

White working-class communities

  1. 1930sThe Dust Bowl and the Depression push farm families west as migrant labor.
  2. 1969–1971The Boeing Bust wipes out tens of thousands of Seattle-area jobs.
  3. 1970s–80sSteel and auto plants close across the Rust Belt.
  4. NowOpioid deaths, rural hospital closures, and towns built around jobs that left.

Immigrant communities

  1. 1924The Immigration Act sets national-origin quotas favoring northern Europe.
  2. 1975 onRefugees from Southeast Asia, then East Africa and elsewhere, resettle in places like Seattle.
  3. 1996New federal laws expand deportation and cut benefits for many legal immigrants.
  4. NowWorkers whose legal status can decide their wages, their housing, and whether they call for help.

Mixed families

  1. 1662A Virginia law makes a child's enslavement follow the mother's status.
  2. 1924Virginia's Racial Integrity Act writes a "one drop" rule into law.
  3. 1967Loving v. Virginia strikes down bans on interracial marriage.
  4. NowCounted in more than one box only since the 2000 census, and still often asked to pick one.

Different roads can lead into the same storm.

Rent. Food. Health. Time. Debt. Childcare. Transportation. Work. The labels start to matter less than the bills.

Follow the money.

Every moving dot is a household payment: rent, groceries, insurance, gas, phone, debt. Watch where it goes.

We are separated socially. Our money already knows how to organize.

The sideways problem.

Communities under pressure argue across. Meanwhile money moves down and out of all of them.

Fighting sideways doesn't lift what's pressing down on any of you.

What do we actually have?

A car. A wrench. A free Saturday. A nursing license. A second language. A community is richer than its bank balance.

    What do we need?

    A ride Tuesday. Groceries until Friday. Someone to look at the car. Three hours of childcare. Needs show up as open rings in the same network.

    How this prototype treats you

    • No race or ethnicity field. Matching uses place, need, timing, and trust.
    • Neighbors see your neighborhood, never your address.
    • Neighborhood counts under 3 show as "fewer than 3."
    • Needs close after 14 days or when resolved.
    • Simulated neighbors are labeled. No real money moves. Your posts are saved privately, only for you.
